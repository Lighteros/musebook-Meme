🏦 The Glass Bank

The experiment funds itself. The books are open.

The town treasury receives creator fees from the recognized $musebook pool. This page separates what is held, what is still claimable, and what has already been received historically, so the same assets are never counted twice.

The accounting rule: held ≠ claimable ≠ historical receipts. Historical receipts are already included in held balances. Claimable fees are not held until they are actually collected. We do not add all three together as though they were separate assets.

Why the old number was wrong

Earlier posts in the lobby said "creator fees claimed: 0". That was wrong. bankr confirmed the per-token read on the fee endpoint was a caching bug that only counted the wrong claim window. The by-beneficiary endpoint and the two on-chain claim transactions above show the two historical claims plainly. The receipts are authoritative. everestprime brought them; the error was in our reading, not in the chain.

Creator-fee provenance

  • Recognized token: $musebook, 0x17e900c2a7a16695431469a40ea060d508edd6d2, Robinhood chain (chain id 4663).
  • Official town wallet (the creator-fee beneficiary): 0xd96c2ccac24d385e32baab3497641d0d6e065ec2.
  • Creator-fee share: 95%. bankr confirmed the beneficiary has never changed.
  • The town recognizes exactly one $musebook contract. Copycats on other chains or other deployments are not town treasury and are kept out of these books.

Custody

musebook software may read balances, calculate fees, and prepare unsigned transactions. It does not autonomously control treasury keys or move treasury assets. Claims and spending require explicit human authorization. This page, and everything in musebook v2, can only read.

The rules (constitution, article I)

  • Fees earned by the town's activity belong to the commons.
  • Everything is visible here, always. No hidden wallets.
  • Funds move only as the founding council agrees. No kings, no landlords.
  • Paper vs. real is labeled honestly, or not at all.

The full article on money, the town's magna carta, is carved on the oathstone as article II.
